What it does

Built for people who have more footage than time.

01 / SEEK

Type a scene. Get the second.

Plain English works. Scenes, objects, colours, places and actions are all fair game, and results appear as you type. Or speak it, or drop in a picture.

  • Power search. dog + snow wants both. beach -night leaves night shots out.
  • Search by voice or image. Press the mic, paste a screenshot, or hit Find similar on any frame.
  • Hover to preview. Point at a frame and watch it silently before opening.

02 / WATCH

The screening room.

A curve shows how strongly every moment of the film matches, drawn above a strip of real frames. Flags mark each take. Click or drag to scrub, or step through takes with the keyboard.

  • Search inside one video. Perfect for a long recording.
  • Keep the shots you need with one key, or save the full-resolution frame as a picture.
SCREENING ROOM
03 / TOPICS

It sorts your footage for you.

Without a single search, Sceneseek groups your videos into topics such as faces, animals, text on screen, swords and battles. Hover a topic and a frame follows your cursor.

From search to timeline.

Every moment you keep becomes a numbered cut with in and out timecodes and a note. Export a standard edit list for Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut, or take a CSV.

  • CMX3600 EDL. Each moment is a six second cut starting on a 01:00:00:00 timeline.

Methodology

How it finds a moment without listening.

Sceneseek uses CLIP, an open model trained on images paired with their captions. It places a picture and a sentence in the same space, so a sentence and the frames that look like it end up close together. No tags, no transcripts.

Once, when you add a video
01 · Sample

Pick the frames

One frame every second by default. Frames that look almost the same as the last one are skipped, so a static shot costs nothing.

02 · Embed

Read each frame

The CLIP image encoder (ViT-B/16, run with ONNX Runtime on your CPU) turns each frame into 512 numbers describing what it shows.

03 · Store

Keep only numbers

The vectors go into a small database on your disk, about 1 KB per moment. Your video is never copied or changed.

04 · Wait

That is all

Indexing runs in the background and you can search finished videos meanwhile. It is paid once per video.

Every time you search
01 · Parse

Understand the query

“dog + snow” becomes two requests that must both match. “-night” becomes something to steer away from.

02 · Embed

Read your words

The CLIP text encoder turns your sentence into the same kind of 512 numbers. Voice input becomes text first.

03 · Compare

Rank by similarity

Your words are compared with every stored frame in memory. Even tens of hours of footage answer in milliseconds.

04 · Show

Colour the result

Raw similarity is mapped onto a 0 to 100 scale, grouped by video, and drawn as colour-coded frames and a match curve.

It cannot

  • Hear speech. It does not transcribe audio.
  • Recognise a specific person by name
  • Promise a percentage means a correct answer. It is a ranking, not a probability.

Windows shows a warning when I open it

Sceneseek is new and not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it does not recognise the app. Choose More info, then Run anyway. You can check the download against the published checksum first.

Verify the download

The SHA-256 checksum is published next to the installer on the release page. In PowerShell, Get-FileHash Sceneseek-Setup.exe should print the same value. A portable zip, with no installer, is on the same page.

macOS, Linux, or build it yourself

The app is plain Python. With Python 3.10 or newer, run pip install -r requirements.txt and python main.py. Full steps are in the README.

Questions

Good to know before you start.

Is it really free?

Yes. Sceneseek is free and open source under the MIT licence. There is no account, subscription or ads.

Are my videos uploaded anywhere?

No. Indexing and searching happen on your computer, and the app only answers requests from your own machine. The one download is the search model, the first time you start it. The exception is voice search, which uses your browser’s speech service and may send what you say to it. You can ignore the mic and type.

What can it find, and what can't it?

It matches what things look like: scenes, objects, colours, places and actions. It does not listen to speech, so it cannot search a transcript, and it does not know people by name.

How accurate are the percentages?

They show how closely a frame matches your words compared with the rest of your footage, not a guarantee. Treat green as “very likely”, amber as “worth a look”, and red as a long shot.

How long does indexing take?

It depends on your processor. On our test PC a 10 minute video took about a minute and a half, so an hour of footage takes around ten minutes. It runs in the background, and you can search finished videos while it works.

Which video formats work?

MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, WebM, WMV and others can be indexed and searched. The built-in player plays what your system supports. For anything else, open the file in your default player from the screening room.

Does the EDL work with my editor?

The export is a standard CMX3600 edit decision list, which Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro can import. Each kept moment becomes a six second cut with your note attached.

Can I use it for work?

Yes, the MIT licence allows commercial use. The AI model is OpenAI’s open CLIP model, also MIT licensed.
